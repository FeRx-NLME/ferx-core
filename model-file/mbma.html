Model-based meta-analysis: start here

A model-based meta-analysis (MBMA) fits a model to published summary data: one mean per trial arm and visit, each with its standard error and arm size. ferx supports it through several features, each documented on its own reference page. This page lists them in the order you meet them when you build such a model, and links to the section that holds the details.

Maturity

Every MBMA-specific piece below is beta: θ level blocks, profile, nested levels, within-arm residual correlation, residual and kappa weight =, and compartment-free models. Each is marked so at the top of its own section, and each has a row in the MBMA group of the Feature Maturity table naming the NONMEM anchor behind the label. Several pieces are limited to method = foce or focei; where a step is limited, it says so.

A minimal model

The pieces fit together like this. The model is ferx-core’s tests/data/mbma_placebo/mbma_placebo.ferx (the same model ferx-r bundles as mbma_placebo), with the optional profile and correlation additions marked:

[parameters]
  theta TVE0(45.0, 0.0, 200.0)      # mean placebo level over a study's visits
  theta EMAX(8.0, 0.0, 100.0)       # maximum drug effect
  theta ED50(20.0, 0.1, 1000.0)     # dose at half-maximal effect
  theta ET50(2.0, 0.01, 100.0)      # time to half-maximal drug effect
  theta PLACEBO[STUDY, TIME, contrast = sum_to_zero_within](0.0, -50.0, 50.0) profile  # `profile` optional
  theta RHO(0.3, 0.0, 0.95)         # optional: only with the correlation line

  omega ETA_E0 ~ 4.0 (sd)           # between-study variability
  kappa KAPPA_ARM ~ 4.0 (sd) weight = NARM   # between-arm variability (BTAV)

  sigma ADD_ERR ~ 1.0 (variance) FIX         # all precision is in the weight

[covariates]
  STUDY categorical
  NARM  continuous
  SE    continuous
  DOSE  continuous

[individual_parameters]
  BASE = TVE0 + PLACEBO + ETA_E0 + KAPPA_ARM

[structural_model]
  DRUG = EMAX * DOSE / (ED50 + DOSE) * TIME / (TIME + ET50)
  y    = BASE - DRUG

[error_model]
  DV ~ additive(ADD_ERR) weight = SE
  correlation car1(RHO) by OCC      # optional

[fit_options]
  method     = focei
  iov_column = OCC
  covariance = true

Without the profile keyword and the two optional lines it is the fixture as committed. With them it parses with ferx check and fits on the fixture’s data.

1. Arm-level data

Each row is one arm of one study at one visit. ID is the study, an occasion column (OCC, named by iov_column) is the arm, and DV is the reported arm mean on its natural scale. The arm’s standard error and size are ordinary data columns (SE, NARM above). In the MBMA layout the arms of a study are its occasions, and each arm carries the study’s visit grid (Kappas). See Required Columns and Occasion Column (IOV).

There are no dose records. The model is a compartment-free model: a [structural_model] with no pk or ode line, whose y = ... equation reads the dose as a data column. The dataset needs no AMT column, and dose records it carries anyway are not applied (What a compartment-free model does not have). A kappa on such a model keeps the analytic gradient up to 48 random effects per subject, counting the stacked η and kappas over its occasions; past that, the fit uses finite differences (Gradients).

In R, ferx_mbma_data() builds and checks such a dataset (Counted form).

2. Inverse-variance weighting

Add weight = SE to the error-model line and fix the additive sigma at 1: each row then contributes \(1/SE^2\) to the objective, and DV, PRED, the sdtab and the VPC stay on the natural scale (Residual weighting). Whether sigma is fixed stays your choice: fix it for a continuous endpoint with a known SE, estimate it for a weighted logit of a proportion (Is sigma fixed?). The reported OFV differs from a hand-built DV/SE fit by a constant that cancels from every ΔOFV (Objective-function value).

3. Between-arm variability

kappa KAPPA_ARM ~ 4.0 (sd) weight = NARM declares an arm random effect with variance \(\gamma^2 / N\), so a large arm’s mean varies less than a small arm’s (Sample-size-weighted IOV). The estimate reported is the unweighted \(\gamma^2\), and the fit prints the SD at the median arm size on the next line (What is reported). The weight may read covariates, FIXed thetas and TIME, and the kappa may be read only in [individual_parameters] (Rules and diagnostics).

4. Placebo time course as a level block

theta PLACEBO[STUDY, TIME](...) declares one fixed effect per (study, visit) cell in the data, so no parametric placebo curve is imposed (Theta level blocks, Data-driven form). Next to an intercept and a study random effect, one degree of freedom per study has to go. contrast = sum_to_zero_within, the default for this shape, makes the levels sum to zero within each study (Identifiability). TVE0 + ETA_E0 is then each study’s mean placebo level over its own visits, and the levels are each visit’s deviation from it. A block of 20 or more coefficients is summarised rather than listed in the estimate table; the derived level of each study is not a coefficient and does not count (Fitting at this scale).

5. Profiled level blocks

Marking the block profile takes its levels out of the optimizer’s search: at every outer evaluation they are solved exactly for the structural parameters being tried. The objective is unchanged, so the OFV, the estimates and a likelihood-ratio test agree with the unprofiled fit (Profiled level blocks). It pays off when the block has many levels; on a small model it can be slower (When to profile). The block must enter every prediction linearly, under foce/focei at n_agq = 1 with an additive, untransformed Gaussian error and no M3 (When it applies). A profiled block takes bounds, and a level held on its bound gets no standard error (Bounds on a profiled block). r, s and rsr all work on a profiled fit (Standard errors).

6. Nested levels

For sponsor → study → arm, put the coarsest grouping in ID and declare the finer ones with level NAME by COLUMN in PARENT, then attach each kappa with kappa ... at LEVEL (Nested levels, Declaring levels). A level replaces iov_column. Every record needs a numeric label at every level (The data). Nested levels are estimated with foce or focei at n_agq = 1 only (What this release supports).

7. Within-arm residual correlation

correlation cs|ar1|car1(RHO) by ARM lets the residuals of one arm’s visits correlate (Within-group residual correlation). It is fitted by foce and focei only (Scope). cs and an arm kappa are the same covariance, so cs over the groups of a kappa-carrying level is refused; with a kappa at the arm, use ar1 or car1 (With kappa and nested levels, Choosing a structure). The line fits next to a profiled level block, and its refusals come first (With a profiled level block).

With few studies, do not read the OFV drop for ρ against a χ² distribution. On the 6-study mbma_placebo data, which the generator draws with no serial correlation, car1(RHO) by OCC lowers the OFV by 7.6. In 50 data sets simulated from the fit without the line (ferx-r ferx_simulate(model, data, n_sim = 50, seed = 1, fit = fit)), each fitted with and without it, the drop was at least 3.84 in 26% of them (macOS arm64, core 32dbb309). A χ²₁ reference puts 5% there; since ρ ≥ 0 places the null on the boundary of its range, the asymptotic reference is the 50:50 χ²₀/χ²₁ mixture, which puts 2.5% there. Calibrate the test by simulating from the model without the line, as the ferx book’s MBMA chapter does in Correlated residuals within an arm.

8. Uncertainty with few studies

The default covariance_method = r gives the model-based SEs (Covariance estimator). When the analytic R-matrix is out of scope and the model has more than 100 free coordinates, a defaulted method routes to s with a warning; set covariance_method = r to keep it (Fitting at this scale). s needs more subjects than free parameters, so a level block with a level per study leaves S rank-deficient and s reports no SEs. On a block whose levels are each read by one subject, the rsr level SEs are not usable; read them from r, and ferx warns with W_COV_RSR_ONE_SUBJECT_LEVELS (Standard errors).

SIR (sir = true, beta) samples from the covariance step’s proposal. It never samples a FIXed sigma, so the fixed residual of the weighting stays fixed (Rank-deficient and non-identified proposals). On the 6-study mbma_placebo fixture the default sir_scale = packed gives a higher effective sample size than natural, whose target has a heavy upper tail for a variance informed by few groups (Choosing the target scale). covariance_fallback = sir runs it when the Hessian is not positive definite (SIR fallback).

After method = saem the covariance step is taken on the FOCEI objective at SAEM’s estimate. On the 6-study mbma_placebo fixture SAEM stops away from that objective’s minimum. The default run then gets no SEs (W_COV_NON_STATIONARY_ESTIMATE), and other seeds report SEs at an equally non-stationary point with no warning. Fit with FOCEI, or chain method = [saem, focei], whose SEs are FOCEI’s at the FOCEI estimate (Standard errors at a SAEM estimate).

9. Bundled examples

  • examples/mbma_naproxen.ferx with data/mbma_naproxen.csv: the published naproxen-versus-placebo MBMA of Boucher & Bennetts (2018), 18 trials and 36 arms, with between-study random effects and no kappa. It keeps the published weighting, written by hand: DV divided in the data, y divided by WPSE, sigma FIX at 1, the construction weight = replaces (Replaces the hand-built three-site construction). data/mbma_naproxen.README.md gives the provenance. The data are licensed CC BY-NC 4.0, not MIT (data/mbma_naproxen.LICENSE): commercial use is not granted.

    ferx examples/mbma_naproxen.ferx --data data/mbma_naproxen.csv

  • tests/data/mbma_placebo/: the synthetic model above, with data. ferx-r bundles it as ferx_example("mbma_placebo"), and the ferx book’s MBMA chapter works through it end to end from R.

    ferx tests/data/mbma_placebo/mbma_placebo.ferx --data tests/data/mbma_placebo/mbma_placebo.csv
